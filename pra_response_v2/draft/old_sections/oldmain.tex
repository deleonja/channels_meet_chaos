\begin{abstract}
We rigorously test the assumption that global quantum chaos can be
detected by the equilibration of a single-spin probe. We benchmark the
mean level spacing ratio against two single-spin dynamical
probes: the averaged state purity and the averaged \jami{}
purity, a metric characterizing the complete reduced
dynamical map. Analyzing the integrability-to-chaos transition in
canonical spin chains, we demonstrate a clear breakdown of this
local-global correspondence. We find that both the averaged state purity
and the averaged \jami{} purity

regimes (e.g., in the XXZ model with a local defect) that spectral

The characterization of quantum chaos in many-body systems is a
fundamental pursuit that bridges quantum mechanics and statistical
mechanics~\cite{bohigas_1984_characterization, deutsch_1991_quantum, srednicki_1994_chaos, rigol_2008_thermalization}.
The onset of chaos is intrinsically linked to
the mechanism of thermalization in isolated quantum systems, as
formalized by the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH)~\cite{dalessio_2016_quantum, deutsch_1991_quantum, srednicki_1994_chaos}.
Understanding this connection is critical for topics ranging from
many-body localization
(MBL)~\cite{nandkishore_2015_manybody, abanin_2019_colloquium, pal_2010_manybody} and the scrambling of quantum
information~\cite{shenker_2014_black, maldacena_2016_bound, swingle_2018_unscrambling, xu_2020_does} to the dynamics of black
holes~\cite{maldacena_2016_bound, balasubramanian_2025_quantum} and
the stability of quantum computation~\cite{georgeot_2000_emergence, c_2025_disordering}.

\section{Models and Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Spin Chain Hamiltonians}\label{sec:spin_chains}
We investigate the transition to quantum chaos in three 
one-dimensional spin-1/2 chain models.

\subsubsection{Mixed-field Ising model}\label{sec:ising}
We investigate the mixed-field Ising model with open boundary 
conditions, described by the Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H:ising}
H = \sum_{i=1}^L \left( h_x \sigma_i^x + h_z \sigma_i^z \right) 
- J \sum_{i=1}^{L-1} \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+1}^z \, ,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_i^\alpha$ ($\alpha \in \{x,y,z\}$) is the Pauli operator 
acting on site $i$, $L$ is the chain length, $J$ denotes the 
nearest-neighbor Ising interaction, and $h_x$ and $h_z$ are the 
transverse and longitudinal field strengths, respectively.

When all parameters are homogeneous, this model possesses a global
spatial reflection symmetry. The corresponding symmetry operator
$\mathcal{R}$, which performs a spatial inversion of the chain
sites ($i \leftrightarrow L-i+1$), commutes with the Hamiltonian,
$[H, \mathcal{R}] = 0$. Consequently, the Hilbert space decomposes
into two independent symmetry sectors, corresponding to even and
odd spatial parity, which are diagonalized separately.

%\cdnote*{muy bien a esto me referia anteriormente jaja}{\color{blue}
The model is integrable in two distinct limits: the transverse-field
Ising model obtained at $h_z = 0$, which is solvable via a Jordan-Wigner
transformation~\cite{sachdev_2011_quantum}; the
classical Ising model recovered when $h_x = 0$~\cite{baxter_2007_exactly}.
%} 
When $J=0$, there are no interactions and the system is reduced to a collection 
of individual spins. 
The simultaneous presence of all three terms make the 
system non-integrable, making the mixed-field Ising model
a paradigmatic model for studying many-body quantum chaos~\cite{kim_2014_testing,garrison_2018_does,borgonovi_2016_quantum}. It has been rigorously proven that for any non-zero
$J$, $h_x$, and $h_z$, the model possesses no non-trivial local
conserved quantities~\cite{chiba_2024_proof}. The chaotic regime is therefore
generic and is frequently benchmarked using parameters where all three
terms are of the same order, $J \sim h_x \sim h_z$, as this maximizes
the non-commutation that drives chaotic behavior~\cite{karthik_2007_entanglement,atas_2017_quantum,camargo_2024_spread}.

\subsubsection{Heisenberg model with random fields}\label{sec:heisenberg}
We next consider the Heisenberg model with random fields,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H:heisenberg}
H = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{L-1} \left(\sigma_i^x\sigma_{i+1}^x
+ \sigma_i^y\sigma_{i+1}^y + \sigma_i^z\sigma_{i+1}^z\right)
+ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^L h_i \sigma_i^z \, ,
\end{equation}
where the $h_i$ are independent random variables drawn uniformly
from the interval $[-h, h]$, and $h$ defines the disorder strength.
This model conserves the total $z$-component of magnetization,
$M_z = \sum_i \sigma_i^z$, as $[H, M_z] = 0$. This conservation
is equivalent to conserving the number of up spins, $N_{\uparrow}$,
where the magnetization is given by $\ev{M_z} = 2N_{\uparrow} - L$.
The Hilbert space thus decomposes into independent sectors, each
labeled by a fixed $N_{\uparrow}$. All spectral statistics are
computed within these fixed-$N_{\uparrow}$ sectors.

The clean limit $h=0$ corresponds to the integrable $SU(2)$ Heisenberg spin
chain, solvable via the Bethe ansatz~\cite{bethe_1931_zur}. For
any $h > 0$, the $SU(2)$ symmetry is broken to $U(1)$, corresponding to the
conservation of $M_z$, and the model becomes 
non-integrable~\cite{santos_2004_integrability}. This Hamiltonian
is a paradigmatic model for studying the transition from a delocalized,
chaotic phase at weak disorder $h < h_c$ to 
%{\color{blue}
the many-body localized \janote{(MBL)}
% \cdnote{SUG: Aquit tal cual si pon Many-Body Localization (MBL) transition, si no la gente de MBL nos van a ahorcar por no llamar el nombre de esa fase integrable, en el texto como lo pusiste parece que ya lo habías mencionado, pero creo que no.}
%}
phase at strong disorder $h > h_c$
~\cite{oganesyan_2007_localization,basko_2006_metal,pal_2010_manybody,luitz_2015_manybody,abanin_2019_colloquium}. 
The critical disorder strength $h_c$ for this transition is estimated to be
$h_c \approx 3.5$~\cite{luitz_2015_manybody}.

\subsubsection{Averaged subsystem purity}\label{sec:avg_P}
The connection between quantum chaos and equilibration in isolated
many-body systems is well-established~\cite{deutsch_1991_quantum,
srednicki_1994_chaos,rigol_2008_thermalization,gogolin_2016_equilibration}.
Chaotic dynamics are expected to make the system act as an effective bath 
for its own subsystems, driving them toward equilibrium states
through canonical typicality~\cite{goldstein_2006_canonical} and under
very general dynamical conditions~\cite{reimann_2008_foundation}. 
Consequently, the degree to which a local probe equilibrates has been 
proposed as a dynamical signature of chaos~\cite{mirkin_2021_quantum}.

In Ref.~\cite{mirkin_2021_quantum}, the integrable-to-chaos transition 
was investigated across several spin-chain models using the purity 
decay of a single probe spin.
The probe is defined as the first spin of the chain, with the remaining
$L-1$ spins forming an effective environment. Starting from a pure
initial state $\rho_{SE}(0)=\dyad{\psi_{SE}(0)}$, the probe's state at
time $t$ is the reduced density matrix 
$\rho_S(t)=\Tr_E(e^{-iHt}\rho_{SE}(0)e^{iHt})$.

$\mathcal{P}(t)=\Tr[\rho_S^2(t)]$, which ranges from $\mathcal{P}=1$
(pure state) to the minimal value $\mathcal{P}=1/2$ for a maximally
mixed state.

proposed averaging the purity over a long time

\subsection{Reduced dynamics as a quantum channel}\label{sec:reduced_dynamics}
%introducir el concepto de canal cuántico como dinámica reducida
The most general physical evolution of a quantum state is described 
by a completely
positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) map $\mathcal{E}$, known as a quantum
channel~\cite{nielsen_2010_quantum,bengtsson_2006_geometry}. We apply this
formalism to the reduced dynamics of the subsystem $S$ (the probe spin)
coupled to an environment $E$ (the rest of the spin chain). The total
system $S+E$ is closed, and its evolution under a time-independent
Hamiltonian $H$ is governed by the unitary operator $U(t) = e^{-iHt}$.

%representación de sistema-entorno
Assuming the total system is initialized in a product state
$\rho_{SE}(0) = \rho_S \otimes \rho_E$ with respect to the
$S|E$ partition, the map from the initial subsystem state $\rho_S$
to the final state $\rho_S(t)$ is represented by a time-dependent
map $\mathcal{E}_t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel_def}
\rho_S(t) = \mathcal{E}_t(\rho_S)
= \Tr_E\left[ U(t) (\rho_S \otimes \rho_E) U^\dagger(t) \right] \, .
\end{equation}
By construction, this map is CPTP
for any choice of the environment state $\rho_E$ and for all
times $t$. Formally, \Eref{eq:channel_def} is referred to as a
system-environment representation of the map $\mathcal{E}_t$. A CPTP map
can have several system-environment representations. This result is
known as the \textit{Stinespring dilation}
theorem~\cite{stinespring_1955_positive}. Moreover,
the theorem also provides conditions for a minimal dilation; for a
subsystem of dimension $d_S$, the minimal environment dimension $d_E$
required to realize any channel $\mathcal{E}$ via Eq.~(\ref{eq:channel_def})
is bounded by $d_E \le d_S^2$~\cite{watrous_2018_theory}. 
In our case, the probe $S$ is a single
spin ($d_S=2$), so any possible channel—from simple unitary rotation
to complete decoherence—can be generated using an environment \mgnote{"effective environment"}\janote{``effecive'' porque querés hacer la diferencia de que no nos referimos al mismo entorno de los $L-1$ espines?} of
dimension at most $d_S^2 = 4$. This implies that any arbitrary channel
$\mathcal{E}_t$, for a fixed $t$, acting on the probe could be realized by
coupling the subsystem $S$ to an environment of dimension as small as
$d_E = 4$---for instance, two spins.

%justificación del formalismo
The quantum channel $\mcE_t$ [c.f. Eq.~(\ref{eq:channel_def})] provides a complete
description of the probe's dynamics, independent of the initial
subsystem state $\rho_S$.

channel $\mcE_t$ acting on the leftmost spin in the mixed-field Ising model ($L=7$)

The quantum channel $\mcE_t$, as defined in \Eref{eq:channel_def}, depends
explicitly on both the unitary evolution $U(t)$ and the initial
environment state $\rho_E$. In our setup, the evolution is governed
by the Hamiltonian $H$ via $U(t) = e^{-iHt}$. We aim to
characterize the generic dynamical properties encoded in $H$---which
determines the system's chaotic or integrable nature---rather than
properties tied to a specific initial state $\rho_E$.
We therefore average over an ensemble of initial environment
states. This procedure ensures our final indicator depends only on the
statistical properties of the chosen ensemble, not on any particular member.

ensuring that the dynamics samples the entire energy spectrum. 

\subsection{Purity of the \jami{} matrix}\label{sec:choi_purity}
% trancisión e introducción de la matriz de CJ
Although the reduced dynamics of the probe defines the
time-dependent quantum channel $\mcE_t$ via a natural system-environment
representation, an alternative representation is often better
suited for analyzing the channel's properties. The CJ
isomorphism provides one such alternative. It establishes that any
CPTP map $\mcE$ corresponds to a quantum state $\mcD$ on an enlarged Hilbert
space~\cite{jamiolkowski_1972_linear,choi_1975_completely}. We
refer to $\mcD$ as the CJ matrix. This channel-state
duality allows concepts from state analysis to be applied
directly to quantum maps.

% Definición formal de la matriz de CJ
The CJ matrix $\mcD$ is obtained by applying the channel $\mcE$
to one part of a maximally entangled state shared between the system $S$
and an identical, isolated ancillary system $S'$. It is formally defined as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:choi_def}
\mcD = (\mcE \otimes \mcI)\qty(\dyad{\Phi^+}) \, .
\end{equation}
Here, $\mcI$ is the identity map acting on the ancilla $S'$, and
$\dyad{\Phi^+} = (1/d_S) \sum_{i,j} \dyad{ii}{jj}$ is the projector onto
the maximally entangled state, with $\{ \ket{i} \}$ being an orthonormal basis
for $S$ (and $S'$). In our case, the probe $S$ is a single spin
($d_S = 2$), and $\dyad{\Phi^+}$ is the projector onto a maximally
entangled Bell state.
The resulting $d_S^2 \times d_S^2$ matrix $\mcD$ is a density matrix
on the composite Hilbert space $S \otimes S'$ that uniquely and completely
characterizes the properties of the channel $\mcE$.
The CJ matrix is also commonly referred to in the literature as the
Choi, Jamiolkowski, or CJ \textit{state}. 
% \mgnote{we should introduce here an abbreviation}\janote{Ok, para seguir los estándares de Phys Rev introduzco
% la abreciación la primera vez que aparece en el texto principal (intro)}

% La pureza de choi captura y diferencia distintos tipos de dinámicas reducidas
The purity of the CJ matrix quantifies intrinsic
properties of the quantum channel $\mcE_t$, allowing it to
distinguish between different types of dynamics.
% \mgnote{this is our key insight into the difference between the chaometer and Choi, we should emphasize it more.}\janote{Eso es lo que hago después en el párrafo...}
In contrast, output-state metrics, such as
the subsystem state purity $\Tr[\rho_S(t)^2]$, only measure the
mixedness of the output.
For instance, consider two distinct processes: (i) a simple unitary
evolution, corresponding to a non-interacting spin in our setup, and
(ii) a single-qubit full amplitude-damping channel, which describes a
system decaying to its ground state by losing energy to the environment.
This latter map is a paradigmatic energy dissipation process that maps the
entire Bloch sphere to the ground state (a single point on its surface).
State purity fails to distinguish these two maps. When acting on any
pure input state, both processes produce a pure output state, yielding
$\Tr[\rho_S(t)^2]=1$. The CJ purity, however,
clearly separates them, yielding $\Tr(\mcD^2) = 1/2$ for the
full damping channel versus $\Tr(\mcD^2) = 1$ for the unitary map. Furthermore, $\Tr(\mcD^2)$ is bounded as $1/d_S^2 \le \Tr(\mcD^2) \le 1$.
The maximum value, $\Tr(\mcD^2)=1$, signifies a unitary evolution, while
the minimum, $\Tr(\mcD^2)=1/d_S^2$, corresponds to the completely
depolarizing channel, which maps any input state to the maximally
mixed state.

Figs.~\ref{fig:cj:purity}(a2) and \ref{fig:cj:purity}(b2) present the dynamical
signatures differentiating two sets of parameters in the mixed-field 
Ising model,
corresponding to regular and chaotic regimes. These regimes are
characterized by the mean level spacing ratio $\expval{\tilde{r}}$
in Figs.~\ref{fig:cj:purity}(a1) and \ref{fig:cj:purity}(b1).
This Bloch sphere dynamics is the effect of the quantum channel
$\mcE_t$. For these two sets of parameters, the different regimes
manifest directly in the reduced dynamics of the probe spin. In
the regular regime, Fig.~\ref{fig:cj:purity}(a2) shows a
quasi-coherent evolution; the accessible volume on the Bloch
sphere remains large, undergoing complex precession with little
deformation. In stark contrast, the chaotic regime
[Fig.~\ref{fig:cj:purity}(b2)] induces rapid decoherence, causing
the Bloch sphere to quickly contract towards a small ellipsoid
revolving around the origin.

We propose to quantify these distinct behaviors of the channel
$\mcE_t$ using the CJ purity, $\Tr[\mcD(t)^2]$. This
metric measures the map's deviation from unitary or its
proximity to complete depolarization. We plot $\Tr[\mcD(t)^2]$ as a
function of time in Figs.~\ref{fig:cj:purity}(a3) and
\ref{fig:cj:purity}(b3). The low-opacity lines correspond to
single initial random product states of the environment. The
fluctuations observed originate from both the properties of the
Hamiltonian $H$ and the specific initial environment state
$\rho_E$ considered.
To isolate the dynamical features attributable primarily to $H$,
we perform an analytical average over the ensemble of initial
product states. Thus, the resulting CJ purity depends on
the generic properties of this ensemble, not on a particular
realization, and reflects the intrinsic properties of the
Hamiltonian.

\qty(\sigma_1^0 \sigma_2^{\alpha_2} \ldots \sigma_L^{\alpha_L})

\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
where $\vec{\alpha} = (\alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_L)$ is a vector of Pauli

This curve provides a representative measure of the dynamics

configurations.

the many-body energy spectrum quantified by $\expval{\tilde{r}}$.
Consequently, the degree of single-spin decoherence should not be
interpreted as a strictly interchangeable proxy for many-body quantum chaos.

We have investigated the validity of single-spin dynamical indicators as
universal proxies for many-body quantum chaos. We benchmarked the
averaged subsystem state purity, $\statep$, and a proposed, more
general indicator---the averaged CJ purity, $\choip$---against the
mean level spacing ratio, $\expval{\tilde{r}}$. Our central finding is
that neither $\statep$ nor $\choip$, despite the latter's
comprehensive description of the reduced dynamics, serves as a
universally reliable proxy for the spectral transition from
integrability to chaos.

Although a strong anticorrelation between the dynamical probes and
